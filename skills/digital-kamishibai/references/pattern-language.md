# Pattern language: holes, and how to fill them

> **A family kamishibai is a story in which a baton passes from someone to someone, over a song.**
> Into it are set the things only this family has — the **holes**.

The skeleton (the story, the science, the history) may be borrowed from outside the home.
The holes can only be dug from inside it. **Where the holes are does not need translating** — only what fills them
differs from family to family. That is why the form can travel while the contents stay private.

Holes and patterns are called by **name**, never by number.

---

## The six holes

| Hole | Whose | What it does | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The child is there** | the child | "I am inside this story" | Naming the child only on a slide of personal thanks or pride. Never point a lesson at a child |
| **The parent's own stumble** (a true story, first person) | the parent | It rings true. **It does not wear out** — it builds into "this parent does not hide things" | First person, failure included |
| **The sounds and things of the house** | the house | "This is ours" | The lightest to gather. A piece a child is practising is played, not talked about |
| **Loss and handing on** | the parent, others | Tears — but **it wears out each time it is used** | Rarely: once in a series, in the closing work. **Never aim for tears** |
| **Real records** (footage, the family's photos and videos) | others, the house | Shows what the story tells; the family's own record reaches **everyone** in the room | Other people's footage by link; the family's record in the ending video |
| **A seat in the making** | the child | A reason to look forward to the show | **Not an interview question.** Invite only. Pushed, it turns into a duty |

### The child is there — naming, and when not to

Putting a child's name into the narration seems the surest way to reach them. It often does the opposite.
What decides is **what the naming does**:

| What it does | Example | Use it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** — "I see you; I am proud of you" | a slide of personal messages near the end | Yes. Save naming for that slide |
| **Address** — "this lesson is for you" | "it is not your fault" said to one child in the room | No. **Pointed at, a child hears an explanation. Not pointed at, the child makes a discovery** |

Ways to give a child a place without naming them:

- **Put them in the picture** (a family meal, drawn, with the child in it — not mentioned).
- Keep out of the narration the child's nickname for themselves, anything that singles them out, a singular "you",
  and the name of what they are practising.

### A seat in the making — why it counts, and why that is dangerous

Children who are half on the making side (keeping a secret about the ending video, adding their own seat to it,
making their own version) look forward to the show in a way none of the other holes produces.
But the moment an adult asks "will you help?", the seat becomes a duty. So the pattern describes only **how to
invite**: keep the seat open, and when the offer is declined, record nothing.

## How holes actually get filled

Each hole is filled in a different way:

| Hole | How it got filled | By whom |
|---|---|---|
| The child is there | **asking the child directly** what they like, at the table, five minutes | the child |
| The sounds of the house | the parent already knew, or a child said it unprompted | the parent's knowledge, the child |
| The parent's own stumble | **asking the parent, with a criterion** ("one that is still unfinished, still unflattering") | the parent |
| Real records | **not asked — found by going through the photo folders**, then the parent fills the gaps | scanning, the parent |
| Loss and handing on | **never asked.** The parent brought it, as the reason for making the work | the parent, unprompted |
| A seat in the making | **always offered by the person themselves** ("I want to make my own version") | the person |

The interview is not only with the parent: the child's place is found by asking the child.
And loss is **not a hole you ask about**. Asking about loss is intrusive; when a parent says why they want to make
the work, it comes up by itself. Pick it up; never dig for it.

### Order of the interview

0. **The way in**: *Why do you want to make this kamishibai? Who do you want to hand what to?*
   (Loss and the parent's own story come up here, unasked.)
1. The sounds of the house — lightest. If the parent knows, do not ask.
2. Real records — **scan, do not ask**. Ask only about missing years.
3. The child is there — **to the child**, at the table, five minutes, without saying why.
4. The parent's own stumble — to the parent, with the criterion "unfinished, unflattering".

- Loss is never asked. A seat in the making is outside the interview.
- **Any hole may stay empty.** The interview is never a condition for starting the script.

### The parent's own stumble — when not to use it

- **Not in a work a child made and narrates.** The work is the child's; a parent's story would take a seat in it.
  Leave the hole out, unless the child asks for it.
- **Not in every work.** Even when the parent narrates, if no story comes to mind, do not squeeze one out. The seat can
  go to a question to the children about their own experience instead.

### Ways the interview went wrong

| What happened | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| The interview became a condition for writing, and stopped the work | **No blockers**: write from what the parent knows now; turn the interview into a five-minute check at the table before the show |
| "There is no record of it" — when there was | Look for existing records before asking |
| The show went on with the questions never asked | Make every hole **optional**: the skeleton must stand with it empty |
| Asking again turned it into a duty | "Later" is an answer. Do not chase |
| A time or number was wrong from the start | Check numbers and names with the person when reading the draft aloud |
| The material had already been used in an earlier work | A home has a limited supply; check what earlier works used |

### What worked

- **Ask without revealing why.** Saying why steers the answer and spoils the surprise at the show.
- **Do not make it homework.** At first only "why this subject?" and "what was fun?". Go further if the person warms to it.
- **Ask before quoting** someone's words in the show.
- **Do not fix the sentence just before a recognition in the script.** The parent says it at the show, in their own words,
  in reply to what the child said. Even when the narrator has rehearsed everything, that one moment stays fresh.
- **Give a criterion when asking for a story** ("unfinished, unflattering"). A failure polished into a success story
  is seen through by children.
- **Do not ask what you do not need.** A work that names no one needs no interview about names; records can be scanned.

## Patterns

Form: **name / context / problem / solution / questions / how it has been filled**.

### The Child Is There

- **Context**: a story made for a child, in which the child has no place.
- **Problem**: naming the child to make sure the story reaches them turns them into the one being lectured.
  The person you most want to reach is the one who braces most.
- **Solution**: keep naming for **recognition** (a personal message). Elsewhere, **put the child in the picture**;
  let what they love or practise be **seen or heard, not talked about**. The sentence just before a recognition is
  not fixed in the script; the parent says it at the show.
- **Questions** (the parent asks the child, at the table, five minutes):
  - "What is your favourite … right now?" "Which scene do you like best?"
  - "What do you like about it?" (the reason, in the child's words, is material for a recognition)
  - A direct question on the work's theme can work too ("Do you think you have a talent?").
    **Do not reveal the purpose.** Ask before quoting the answer.
- **How it has been filled**: the vehicle or scene the child named, on the cover and in the opening; the child's answer
  to a direct question, quoted on a personal-message slide; a family meal drawn with the child in it, never mentioned.

### The Parent Falls First

- **Context**: a parent wants to tell a child "try", "keep going".
- **Problem**: said straight, it is a sermon. A success story is a boast; a failure tidied into a lesson is seen through.
- **Solution**: the parent offers **one true story, first person, unfinished and unflattering**. Do not wrap it up as a
  lesson; tell how it felt. Used again and again, it does not wear out — it builds trust.
- **Questions** (to the parent, with the criterion):
  - "One detour you thought was a waste, that matters now."
  - "Something you gave up or could not keep up. Why?"
  - "A wall you have still not fully climbed."
  - Criterion: **unfinished, unflattering**. Not a story that ended well.
- **Related**: **Narrator in the Dock** — the teller opens their own side's fault first, so advice becomes a confession.
- **How it has been filled**: a winding career told as what it gave in the end; a setback at work told as still
  unfinished; the parent's own abandoned lessons, listed honestly.

### Play the Home Sound

- **Context**: the borrowed story still sounds like somebody else's.
- **Problem**: introducing a child's favourite song in the narration is just an explanation. A song with lyrics under
  the narration drowns it.
- **Solution**: play a song someone in the family loves, practises or recommended, **where there is little narration**.
  Do not point at a piece someone is practising; just play it.
- **Questions** (the lightest; ask first):
  - "What is playing at home these days?" "What is … practising now?"
  - "Has anyone recommended a song to you?" — a song passed **from a child to a parent** is especially strong.
  - The parent often knows already. **If you can write without asking, do.**
- **How it has been filled**: the piece a child was practising as an act's music; a song a child recommended to the
  parent, at the peak; the opening song chosen at a child's request.

### Family Record on One Song (the ending video)

- **Context**: after the story, the family should carry it home as their own.
- **Problem**: there are hundreds or thousands of family photos; picking by hand is so heavy that it never gets done.
- **Solution**: **photos and videos from birth until now — pets too — laid over one song.** Let a tool go through the
  folders; the parent fills only the missing years. The same material works again in another work, with another song.
- **Questions**: scan before asking. Ask only about gaps ("Are there other photos from that year?"), while watching.
- **How it has been filled**: one song's worth chosen from a few hundred photos and videos; from a few hundred videos of
  lessons; a pet's old footage that made the room laugh again in a later work.

### The Baton Passes

- **Context**: a parent has something they want to remain after they are gone.
- **Problem**: placed to cause tears, loss turns the gift into a device. Used every time, children brace and it wears out.
- **Solution**: show the moment when someone is lost and what was theirs (words, a song, a role) **passes to someone
  else**. At a farewell, play **the song of the one who receives**. Once in a series, in the closing work.
  **Never aim for tears.**
- **Questions**: **none. Do not ask.** When the parent tells why they want to make the work, pick up what comes.
- **How it has been filled**: a parent brought "I want to hand on this person's favourite saying" as the reason for
  the work; one line about the parent's own finite time, passed at once to what the next generation does;
  a handing-on visible in public footage.

### Keep a Seat Open

- **Context**: a child begins to show interest in the work.
- **Problem**: "help me" or "would you like to narrate?" connects the circuit of duty. A yes that comes from it carries
  no information.
- **Solution**: keep seats open (a seat in the ending video, narrating, bringing a subject) and **wait for the offer**.
  When it comes, **hand the decisions to that person** (adults and AI offer candidates, checks and drafts). The date is
  theirs too. If declined, record nothing.
- **Questions**: **none.** There is only the answer to an offer: "Great. What would you like to do?"
- **How it has been filled**: a child who shared the secret of the ending video waited eagerly for the show;
  a child who said "I want to make it" built the ending video's structure; "I want to make my own version";
  volunteering to narrate; family members bringing their own subjects.

### Related patterns (not holes)

| Name | In short |
|---|---|
| **Narrator in the Dock** | the teller opens their own side's fault first |
| **The Receiver's Song** | at a farewell, the song of the one who takes over (joins Play the Home Sound and The Baton Passes) |
| **Narrator Handover** | when a child takes over the narrating, the parent moves backstage (pictures, music) |
| **Whoever Brings It Decides** | parent or child, the maker decides; AI offers candidates, checks and drafts |
| **A Skeleton That Stands With Empty Holes** | the interview is never a precondition; without an answer, drop the seat and perform |

## Open questions

- Most of these questions worked in the hands of one experienced maker. Whether another parent gets the same answers
  with the same questions is not yet known.
- Some children do not answer ("later"). Does "the child is there" stand on pictures and sound alone?
- Should "a seat in the making" be published as a hole at all? Read by another family as "get your child to help",
  it would work as a duty. This text describes only how to invite.
