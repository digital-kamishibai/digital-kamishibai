# Sources — Six Friends Smoothie

The science in the story was checked against the sources below. The numbers were used for checking; the story does
not say them.

| What the story says | What checking found | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| In ancient China there is a legend about a man who ate pine nuts and became an immortal (slide 2) | The story of Wo Quan in the *Liexian Zhuan* (Biographies of Immortals). A legend, not evidence of a long life, so the word "legend" stays | *Liexian Zhuan*, Wo Quan |
| Pine nuts carry vitamin E (slide 2) | Pine nuts are high in vitamin E (about 9 mg per 100 g) | USDA FoodData Central |
| Cashew nuts carry iron (slide 2) | Cashews and pine nuts are high in iron (about 5.5–6.7 mg per 100 g) | USDA FoodData Central |
| Golden kiwifruit has much more vitamin C than green kiwifruit (slide 2) | About 161 mg per 100 g in a common golden variety, about 83 mg in green kiwifruit. There are several golden varieties, so the story does not say "twice" | Richardson DP et al. (2018) *Nutrients* |
| The tomato's red is lycopene. Lycopene is thought to help keep the body from rusting (slide 3) | Lycopene is the red pigment of tomatoes (a carotenoid). In a test tube it is a strong antioxidant. How well it works in the body is still being studied, so "is thought to" stays | Unlu NZ et al. (2005) *Journal of Nutrition*; reviews of carotenoids as antioxidants |
| Lycopene dissolves in oil. With the nuts' oil it gets into the body more easily (slide 3) | Correct. Eating it with fat greatly increases absorption (a study that added avocado to salsa). The story does not give the number | Unlu NZ et al. (2005) *Journal of Nutrition* |
| Cashew oil has lots of oleic acid, the same oil as in olive oil (slide 3) | Correct. A little over half of the fat in cashews is monounsaturated, mostly oleic acid. Pine nut oil is mostly linoleic acid, so the story does not say "nut oil is oleic acid" in general | USDA FoodData Central (cashew nuts, raw) |
| The iron in nuts and beans isn't good at getting into the body; vitamin C lends a hand (slide 3) | Correct. Iron from plants is absorbed poorly, and soybeans also contain phytate, which gets in the way. Vitamin C in the same meal helps absorption | Hurrell RF et al. (1992) *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*; studies of iron absorption by Hallberg L et al. |
| When vitamin E gets tired from protecting the body, vitamin C is thought to give it its energy back (slide 3) | Clear in a test tube. It has been seen in people too, but how strongly varies from tissue to tissue. "Is thought to" stays | Packer L et al. (1993), review of the interaction of vitamins E and C |
| There are studies showing tomatoes and nuts help blood vessels stretch and widen (slide 4) | A review of 21 trials of eating tomatoes found improved blood vessel widening (flow-mediated dilation, FMD). Studies of nuts point the same way. The story never says "cleans your blood vessels" | Cheng HM et al. (2017) *Molecular Nutrition & Food Research*; a meta-analysis of nuts and blood vessel function (*Nutrients*, 2021); Estruch R et al. (2018) *New England Journal of Medicine* |
| Things that keep the body from rusting seem to be stronger as a team than one taken out on its own (slide 4) | A review of trials of single antioxidant supplements (67 trials, about 230,000 people) found they did not help people live longer. Eating vegetables and fruit of many colors is linked with better protection of the body. The story never says "supplements are bad" | Bjelakovic G et al. (2012) *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews* CD007176 |
| The years when you can move around full of energy are your healthy years (slide 4) | Healthy life expectancy: the years lived without health problems limiting daily life | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare of Japan, e-health net, "Healthy life expectancy" |
| The pulp is fiber. It becomes food for the bacteria in your tummy and helps keep your tummy working well (slide 5) | Correct. Fiber reaches the large intestine, where gut bacteria break it down. Its effect on regular bowel movements is well established. The story does not say smoothies are better than juice | Makki K et al. (2018) *Cell Host & Microbe*; Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare of Japan, e-health net, "Dietary fiber" |
